Artists prefer a wide range of surfaces for painting, drawing, printmaking, and mixed media. We choose each surface for its quality and durability. Whether you're a new artist or have years of experience, you'll find the perfect fit for your style.

Canvases

Our stretched canvases come in cotton, linen, and other materials. They are ideal for oils, acrylics, and mixed media, and most are ready to paint right away.

Canvas panels are lightweight, making them perfect for outdoor work or classroom use. If you need something substantial or want more control over your canvas size, our canvas rolls let you cut and stretch to your exact needs.

Art Papers

We carry a variety of drawing and sketching papers, ideal for pencils, charcoal, or markers. If you love watercolor, our watercolor papers are designed to handle water-based paints without warping.

For more detailed work with ink or markers, Bristol paper is a popular choice. We also have specialty papers for calligraphy, pastel, and mixed media so that you can find the right surface for any technique.

Boards and Panels

Our illustration boards are smooth and sturdy, making them suitable for detailed designs or precise artwork. Gessoed panels come pre-primed, so you can start painting with acrylics or oils as soon as you open the package.

If you prefer a more natural base, wood panels can be left uncoated or primed with gesso for a smoother finish. These panels are strong and less likely to warp.

Unique Surfaces

Yupo paper is a slick, synthetic material that helps watercolors and ink flow in interesting ways. Scratchboard has a top layer you can scratch away to reveal a different color beneath, letting you create dramatic black-and-white or colored pieces.

If you enjoy printmaking, explore our printmaking surfaces designed for techniques like relief printing, lithography, and screen printing.

How to Choose the Right Surface?

Think about the medium you use most.

Painting with Oils or Acrylics

Stretched canvases or gessoed panels often work best. They provide a stable, primed surface that holds paint well and supports layering.

Painting with Watercolors

Watercolor painters usually need paper made for watercolor because it can handle moisture better without warping or buckling.

Surface Texture

Some artists prefer a smooth surface, while others need more texture or tooth for their style. Rougher surfaces grip dry media like charcoal or pastel, while smoother ones suit fine detail work.

Project Size

Consider the size of your project. Small panels work well for quick studies, while large canvas rolls suit mural-sized art and give you full control over dimensions.

Tips for Caring for Your Surface

  • Store paper flat or in a protective folder so it stays smooth.
  • Keep canvases and panels upright in a dry area to avoid damage.
  • If you want a smoother canvas, add extra layers of gesso and lightly sand each layer before the next.
  • Proper storage and care help your art materials last longer.
