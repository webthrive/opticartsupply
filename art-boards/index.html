Art Boards

Art Boards

Art boards provide you with a hard, flat, drawing, painting, mounting, and clean presentation surface. They can be bent, which makes the edges, details, and finished pieces look professional. They make an excellent choice in studios, in classrooms that include design tables, in framing, and where it suits you to have your work flat in order to scan it, display it, or sell it.

What Are Art Boards?

An art board is a stiff, non-concave but supportive board that maintains artwork firm, as well as cleaner and less warped. Art boards are hard stands that are used to keep artwork in place. They minimize bending, enhance handling, and safeguard fragile surfaces in the working and handling process. There are also many boards which have surfaces prepared to take certain media, such as smooth faces to take ink and marker, or primed surfaces to take paint.

Art boards are not like canvas and sketch paper since they are stiff. You should use an art board when you require firmer support or cleaner mounting, to have a smoother finish, or when the piece should remain flat as it is housed in the frame or portfolio.

Different Types of Art Boards

Art boards come in several main types, from lightweight foam cores to dense paper boards and durable metal panels for different media and finishes.

White Foam Board

White foam board is an easily portable board that has a foam core and a smooth paper surface. It is produced by pasting layers of paper on a polystyrene center that is rigid.

Best uses: Rapid prototyping and demos, framing prints for a light display, and cutting stencils and clean templates.

Pros: Light and simple to trim, smooth surface for neat layouts.

Limits: Dents easily, especially on corners. Can warp with moisture.

Aluminum Artist Panel

An aluminum artist panel is a hard metal surface to be used as a long-term, stable painting surface. It is made of an aluminum sheet or composite material, after which it is coated or primed to have paint adhered.

Best uses: Acrylic paintings with sharp detail, oil painting on a firm support, mixed media with heavy texture, and archival work that must stay flat.

Pros: Very stable and warp-resistant, great for clean smooth finishes.

Limits: Higher cost than paper boards. Some media may need extra priming.

Mounting Boards

Mounting boards are dense paper-based boards used to support and present finished work. They're made by compressing layered paper fibers into a stiff, flat sheet.

Best uses: Matting and framing support, mounting photos and art prints, backing for finished drawings, portfolio presentation and protection.

Pros: Creates a clean, professional look. Helps artwork stay flat.

Limits: Slim sheets may get bent in the course of manipulation. Types not archival can discolour with time.

Poster Board

The poster board is a heavy paper board used in displays and signs, and in school work. It is created through layering paper stock and pushing it into a smooth sheet that is solid.

Best uses: School displays and posters, signs, charts, and layouts, simple crafts and cutouts, quick marker or pen designs.

Pros: Affordable, good for writing and drawing.

Limits: Not ideal for wet paint. Creases and warps more easily.

Bristol Board

Bristol board is a smooth and heavy paper board on which clean illustration work is done. It is produced by gluing a series of paper layers to create a bright and strong piece of paper.

Best uses: Ink drawings and line art, marker work and smooth blending, colored pencil with sharp detail, lettering and calligraphy practice.

Pros: Smooth surface for clean lines. Holds detail better than sketch paper.

Limits: Some markers can bleed through. Heavy washes can buckle it.

Shikishi Boards

Shikishi boards are artistic boards used in brushing and holding displays of Japanese art. They are created using a layered board and paper, usually with a stylish edge of gold.

Best uses: Sumi-e and brush ink art, light watercolor studies, calligraphy and decorative lettering, small display-ready gift pieces.

Pros: Presentation-ready look, great for brush techniques.

Limits: Smaller standard sizes. Too much water can warp it.

Art Board Sizes, Thickness, and Finishes

Popular sizes of art boards are 9x12, 11x14, 14x17, 16x20, and 18x24. Most are an approximate size of a popular mat and frame, and so they are easier to finish. Presentations and display boards will also be done on larger sheets.

Thickness may range from thin boards used for light mounting to thick panels used during painting. Thickness is most crucial with wet media, rich texture, and frequent handling. During storage and transportation, thinner boards are also favored.

Finishes vary by board type:

  • Smooth surfaces work best for ink, marker, and clean detail.
  • Vellum and toothy surfaces help with graphite and colored pencil control.
  • Textured or primed surfaces help paint grip and improve the final look.

How to Choose the Right Art Board?

Choose Based on Medium

  • Acrylic and Oil: Primed panels, wood panels, or aluminum panels
  • Ink and Marker: Smooth Bristol or illustration board
  • Colored Pencil and Graphite: Bristol (smooth or vellum), drawing board
  • Mounting and Framing: Acid-free mounting board

Choose Based on Skill Level

  • Learner: Foam board, poster board, or Bristol - affordable and easy to handle.
  • Student: Bristol or mounting board for cleaner work and better storage.
  • Professional: Primed boards, aluminum panels, or acid-free boards for finish and longevity.

Choose Based on Final Use

  • Framing: Customized-size acid-free boards.
  • Display: Poster board and foam board for temporary display material.
  • Practice: Non-archival, lower-cost boards are fine for tests and drills.
  • Selling art: Archival boards that are rigid, do not curve, and can be photographed.

How to Store and Care for Art Boards

Store art boards flat in a dry, clean space, protect edges, and avoid heat, direct light, and sudden moisture changes. Good storage keeps boards from bending, swelling, yellowing, or picking up dents. It also helps your artwork stay smooth and ready for framing, scanning, or selling.

  • Store boards flat between clean surfaces to avoid scuffs.
  • Store boards in a dry place, not near humidity, windows, or damp spaces.
  • Carry a hard portfolio case to guard corners while traveling.
  • Seal boards used with wet media to minimize warping.
  • Do not incline thin boards more than necessary.

Final Thoughts

  • Art boards enhance a cleaner, more stable, and controlled surface of work.
  • The best board depends on your medium, finish goals, and how you plan to display it.
  • Thickness and texture can vary on the surface, altering the behavior of your lines and paint.
  • Framing, storing, and selling finished works are safer when using acid-free boards.
